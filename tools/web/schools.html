T-MINUS for schools

Students aged 12 and up build a CubeSat and a rocket part by part, launch it, and learn from a debrief that names the cause of every failure. It runs in the browser.

What is free, for everyone, always

  • The whole game, all missions and the Space Guide, for any number of students.
  • No accounts, no ads, no tracking. Progress and results stay on the student's own computer. Anonymous usage stats are off unless a student turns them on.
  • The class results page: students export their results as text, you paste them in, and see scores, who reached orbit and which causes lost the most missions. Nothing is uploaded.
  • Remote crew play (up to four roles on four computers) and the Failure Lab repair jobs, which make good ten-minute starters.

The school pilot

We are looking for two or three schools to use T-MINUS in real lessons this term. In return we ask for honest feedback: what worked, what confused students, and what you would want from a paid school licence (for example a live class dashboard, saved class rosters, or support for your curriculum).

  • You get: help setting up, a lesson plan for two periods, and a say in what we build next.
  • We ask for: a short written reflection from the teacher, and, if students agree, their anonymous result files.
  • Price: nothing during the pilot. Any school licence will be decided with the pilot schools, and everything above stays free.

What we need to run it well

  • A desktop browser (Chrome, Edge or Firefox) with a keyboard and mouse. Touch screens are experimental.
  • About 26 MB to load on first use; the browser keeps it afterwards. It also works offline as a Windows download.
  • The in-game helper (HOUSTON) works offline. Its optional AI answers send the typed question to an AI service, and a teacher can switch it to hints only or off in Settings.
